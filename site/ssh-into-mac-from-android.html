How to SSH into your Mac from an Android phone

You can open a terminal on your Mac from an Android phone anywhere in the world, without opening any ports on your router. This guide shows the do-it-yourself route using Tailscale, then the shortcut.

What you need

  • A Mac that stays awake and online (see keeping it awake).
  • A free Tailscale account. Tailscale builds a private network between your own devices, so the Mac does not have to be exposed to the internet.
  • An SSH app on the phone, such as Termux or ConnectBot.

1. Turn on Remote Login on the Mac

Open System Settings > General > Sharing and switch on Remote Login. This lets you sign in to the Mac over SSH with your Mac user name and password.

2. Put the Mac and the phone on the same Tailscale network

Install Tailscale on the Mac and on the phone and sign in to the same account on both. Each device gets a private name and an address starting with 100.. They can now reach each other from any network, including mobile data.

3. Connect from the phone

In your SSH app, run this with your own Mac user name and the Mac’s Tailscale name or address:

ssh yourname@your-mac

The first time, you confirm the Mac’s host key and type your password. You now have a normal Mac terminal.

The catch. If the Mac is asleep it will not answer. Closing the lid normally puts it to sleep, which is why the Mac has to be kept awake first.

Making it nicer

  • Use keys instead of a password. Add your phone’s public key to ~/.ssh/authorized_keys on the Mac.
  • Keep programs running when the phone drops. Start tmux on the Mac and attach to it, so a lost connection does not stop what you were doing.
  • Mobile data is fine. Tailscale finds a direct path or relays through its servers.

Want the short version?

Remote Wake does all three steps for you. The Mac app installs the stay-awake service and Tailscale, the Android app has a real terminal and a file browser built in, and you do not need to set up Remote Login, passwords or keys.
