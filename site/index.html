Made for leaving your Mac behind

Awake with the lid closed

A small background service keeps the Mac running while it is plugged in. On battery it sleeps normally.

Private by design

Your phone and Mac talk directly over Tailscale. There is no Remote Wake account and no server of ours in the middle.

A real terminal

Full-screen programs, an extra keys row for Esc, Tab and Ctrl, and automatic reconnect when the network drops.

Questions

Does my Mac stay awake on battery?

No. It stays awake only while plugged in. On battery it sleeps like normal, which protects your battery.

What is Tailscale and do I need an account?

Tailscale makes the private link between your devices. You need a free account, and you sign in once on the Mac and once on the phone with the same account.

Every so often my phone asks me to approve a connection.

That is Tailscale's safety check, about every 12 hours. The app shows an Approve button that opens the approval page.

Claude Code or another tool asks me to log in on the phone?

Tools like Claude Code keep their login in the Mac's Keychain, which a phone connection cannot open. The Mac app solves this with Phone sessions, which are always on: phone terminals join a session in your desktop login, where those saved logins work. The app adds a short, marked block to your ~/.zshrc (delete it and the app adds it back at the next start) and needs the Mac to be logged in (a locked screen is fine).

The Mac asked me to allow access, and I am away from it?

macOS only shows those prompts on the Mac’s own screen, so a phone session cannot answer them. Setup has a step for this: switch on Remote Wake Menu in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Full Disk Access once, while you are at the Mac. After that, programs you run from the phone can read your files without asking. Full Disk Access is broad, so only do this on a Mac you trust the phone with. After each update of the Mac app, macOS forgets the setting and you switch it on again.

What if the connection drops?

The app reconnects by itself. Phone sessions are always on in the Mac app, so your programs keep running and you land back in the same session.

What happens after a power cut?

If FileVault is on, the Mac waits for its password at startup, and nothing can reach it until someone unlocks it.

Is there an iPhone app?

Not yet.
