Can you wake a sleeping Mac from your Android phone?

Not reliably, and not over the internet. We tried the two obvious ways on a real MacBook and neither woke it. What does work is making sure the Mac never falls asleep in the first place.

Why waking a Mac is hard

A sleeping Mac switches off almost everything, including most of the parts that listen to the network. Wake-on-LAN apps send a “magic packet” to a computer on the same local network. That is not the same as reaching a Mac from a phone on mobile data, and it is usually more dependable over Ethernet than over Wi-Fi.

What we tested

One MacBook Pro (Apple M5 Pro, macOS 26), home Wi-Fi, October 2026. Each test asked: while the Mac is asleep, can something from the internet wake it within a minute?

  • A push notification (APNs). The Mac did not wake. Before it sleeps it tells Apple’s push servers to hold messages, and then it only checks in again on its own schedule, roughly every 10 to 15 minutes. The push we sent was delivered only after we woke the Mac by hand.
  • A network packet on an open connection. Also no wake. About eight minutes later the Mac was still asleep and silent. Setting the TCP keepalive-offload flag as root did not change that.
  • Waking the Mac every 30 seconds on a schedule and asking a server for news. This did work, on AC and on battery, with the lid open and closed. But it needs a root helper and it costs battery. We never measured how much, so we did not build the product on it.
Limits of these tests. One Mac, one network, a few trials each. We did not test Wake-on-LAN magic packets or a wired connection, so we are not claiming that Wake-on-LAN never works for you.

What works instead: do not let it sleep

If the Mac is awake, your phone can simply connect to it. That means two things: keep the Mac awake (see how to keep a Mac awake with the lid closed) and have a private path to it from anywhere (see how to SSH into your Mac from Android).

The cost is power. In one overnight test, a Mac left unplugged and idle with the lid closed went from 100% to 94% in about 15 hours. That is one measurement, not a promise, and a Mac under load drains much faster. Plugged in, there is no drain to worry about.

Two things to know

  • If the battery runs out, the Mac shuts down and nothing can reach it until someone plugs it in.
  • After a power cut, a Mac with FileVault turned on waits for its password at startup, so it cannot be reached until someone unlocks it.

Want the short version?

Remote Wake keeps your Mac awake with the lid closed and gives your Android phone a terminal and file browser for it, over Tailscale. It does not wake a sleeping Mac, it makes sure the Mac never needs waking.
