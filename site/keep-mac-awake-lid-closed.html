How to keep your Mac awake with the lid closed

By default a MacBook goes to sleep when you close the lid. Here are the ways to stop that, from the built-in one to the Terminal commands, and what each one costs you.

1. Clamshell mode (built in)

Plug the Mac into power, connect an external display and a keyboard or mouse, then close the lid. macOS treats the external display as the screen and keeps running. It is the supported way, but it needs a display.

2. caffeinate -s (on AC power)

In Terminal, caffeinate -s keeps the Mac from sleeping for as long as the command runs, while it is plugged in. In our tests it kept a MacBook awake with the lid closed on AC. Press Control-C to stop it.

caffeinate -s

3. pmset disablesleep (including on battery)

On battery, caffeinate does not stop the lid from putting the Mac to sleep. A system setting does:

sudo pmset -a disablesleep 1

This is a persistent system setting. It stays on after a restart until you undo it:

sudo pmset -a disablesleep 0
Do not forget to switch it back. A MacBook that never sleeps will stay on in a bag and can get warm. Never run a closed laptop somewhere it cannot shed heat.

What it costs

  • Battery. In one overnight test, an idle Mac unplugged with the lid closed went from 100% to 94% in about 15 hours. A busy Mac drains much faster.
  • When the battery runs out the Mac shuts down.
  • Wi-Fi. It must be a network the Mac rejoins by itself, with no login page.

Third-party apps such as Amphetamine also offer a lid-closed option, if you prefer a menu bar switch to Terminal commands.

Want the short version?

Remote Wake is a single switch in a Mac app. It keeps the Mac awake with the lid closed, plugged in or on battery, resets the setting when you turn it off, and warns you about the battery.
